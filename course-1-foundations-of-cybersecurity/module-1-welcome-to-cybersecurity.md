# Module 1: Welcome to the Exciting World of Cybersecurity

## Key Concepts

- **Cybersecurity** is the practice of ensuring the confidentiality, integrity, and availability (CIA) of information by protecting networks, devices, people, and data from unauthorized access by threat actors.
- **Threat actors** are individuals or groups who present a security risk by attempting to compromise systems. Threats can be **internal** (from within the organization) or **external** (from outside).
- Security teams defend against both internal and external threats. An organization's security is considered effective when it:
  - Meets regulatory compliance requirements
  - Reduces expenses associated with security risks/breaches
  - Improves brand trust with customers and users

## Role of an Entry-Level Security Analyst

- Core responsibility: **monitoring and protecting** information and systems within the organization.
- Analysts are the **first responders** to detected threats.
- They strengthen security posture by collaborating across teams:
  - **IT team** — installing prevention software
  - **Pentesting team** — identifying vulnerabilities
  - **Development team** — integrating security into the software/hardware development lifecycle
- Analysts also perform **periodic security audits** to review the organization's overall security.

## Key Terminology

| Term | Definition |
|---|---|
| **Compliance** | The process of adhering to internal standards and external regulations (e.g., government laws) to avoid fines and security breaches |
| **Security framework** | Guidelines used to build plans that mitigate risks and threats to data and privacy (e.g., NIST CSF, NIST RMF, HIPAA, ISO, SOC 2) |
| **Security controls** | Safeguards designed to reduce specific security risks; used alongside frameworks to build a strong security posture (e.g., strong passwords, antivirus software, encrypting sensitive data) |
| **Security posture** | An organization's ability to manage the defense of its critical assets/data and react to change |
| **Network security** | The practice of keeping an organization's network secure from unauthorized access |
| **Cloud security** | Ensuring assets stored in the cloud are properly configured and accessible only to authorized users |
| **Programming** | Creating a specific set of instructions for a computer to execute tasks — e.g., automating repetitive tasks, alerting on suspicious activity, reviewing web traffic |

## Why Security Matters

Data breaches can harm an organization's reputation, as well as the lives and reputation of its users, clients, and customers. Data at risk generally falls into two categories:

- **PII (Personally Identifiable Information)** — e.g., date of birth, name, email address, phone number
- **SPII (Sensitive Personally Identifiable Information)** — e.g., medical/financial information, biometrics, government ID numbers

If stolen, this data creates a real risk of **identity theft** — which is why security professionals exist to protect data, products, and people.
