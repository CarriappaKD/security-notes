# Module 5: Use AI to Modify Workflows

## AI in Cybersecurity

**Artificial intelligence (AI)** refers to computer programs that can complete cognitive tasks normally associated with human intelligence. AI tools can augment and automate general work — drafting emails and documents, summarizing information, and helping analyze data. In cybersecurity, they help professionals analyze data, streamline communication, and make informed decisions.

**AI cuts both ways.** As AI systems become more common, they become attractive targets for malicious actors, and cybercriminals can use AI to launch more sophisticated attacks, evade detection, and exploit weaknesses. Security professionals need to understand AI's potential for both good and harm, and learn how to secure AI systems and stay ahead of those who misuse them.

## What Generative AI Can Do

**Generative AI** is a type of AI that creates new content, and it can help with both practical and creative tasks.

| Use | Example |
|---|---|
| **Create content** | Generate text, images, or other media — e.g., a large set of fake data to test your organization's security tools, or a list of cybersecurity best practices for colleagues |
| **Analyze information quickly** | Summarize security reports or meeting transcripts to surface key details faster |
| **Answer questions in detail** | Research topics like common threat types (malware, ransomware) |
| **Simplify day-to-day work** | Get an initial read on whether an email shows signs of phishing |
| **Scan code** | Check for common errors, vulnerabilities, and performance bottlenecks |
| **Assist detection and response** | Help investigate alerts |
| **Vibe code custom apps** | Build custom tools to share with your team and streamline work |

## The Prompting Framework: T-C-R-E-I

A **prompt** is the input given to an AI model to produce a response. The mnemonic: **T**houghtfully **C**reate **R**eally **E**xcellent **I**nputs.

| Letter | Element | What to Do |
|---|---|---|
| **T** | **Task** | Clearly define what the model should do. Two parts: **Persona** — the role, expertise, or perspective the AI should take, plus the intended audience; and **Format** — how the output should look (bulleted list, table, short sentences) |
| **C** | **Context** | Provide the details and background the AI needs to understand your specific situation |
| **R** | **References** | Include examples, past work, or source material to guide the response, when applicable |
| **E** | **Evaluate** | Check whether the output meets your goal and is accurate |
| **I** | **Iterate** | If the output falls short, refine the prompt or add more information |

The framework is **tool agnostic** — it works across tools such as Gemini, ChatGPT, Claude, and Copilot.

## Responsible Use of Generative AI

AI tools have limitations, so use them responsibly:

- **Review outputs carefully** for accuracy and usefulness — AI lacks human practical judgment and experience
- **Disclose your use** of generative AI
- **Consider privacy and security implications**
  - Never input confidential, sensitive, or personal information
  - Follow your organization's security policies and read the terms of service to see how your data is handled
  - Check that you're using an **approved, secure enterprise environment** before uploading sensitive or confidential data
- **Make sure custom-built AI apps are safe and secure** before sharing them with others
- **Keep a human in the loop** — AI should complement human skills, not replace them

> This list isn't exhaustive. Always check your company's own policies on generative AI.
